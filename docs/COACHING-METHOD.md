# Deepika's method, as she wrote it

Written 28 November 2026 from two things Deepika made for herself: the workbook
she ran her mock first call from (four sheets), and the call itself, which is
logged as Round 2 in `docs/PILOT-FEEDBACK.md`. The workbook is headed "Draft (to
evolve)", so this is where her method stood that day, not a specification.

Read this before changing onboarding, goals, the assessment, or anything else a
member is asked about herself. The app was built before her method was written
down, and the table at the end shows where the two differ.

**What is not here.** The workbook was filled in for one client. Those answers
are personal and are not in this repository. Only her questions and the shape of
her sheets are. The file itself stays with Eshan.

She says "client"; the app says "member". Both are used below, each for its own
side.

---

## What she promises: "Project You"

Six lines, on a sheet of their own.

1. Longevity: a long, happy and healthy life.
2. The whole person, across the different dimensions of health.
3. Slow and sustainable. Growth is not linear, and life happens.
4. A safe place, with no judgement.
5. Collaborative: you are in the driving seat and I guide you.
6. A woman's life stage.

She plans to show them on one slide before the first call, so a client knows
she is in the right hands. Her note for that slide adds: happy and healthy by
the 80:20 rule, health habits fitted into life and not the other way round, and
no blame.

## Three moments

### 1. Before the call: the intake form

Twenty questions in seven sections. She would rather it were filled in
beforehand, which makes the call 30 to 45 minutes instead of 45 to 60.

| Section | She asks |
| --- | --- |
| General | Name · Age · Gender · Weight · Height · Relationship status · Number of children and their ages |
| Health | Current health goals · Any health issues · Exercise or activity enjoyed in the past |
| Lifestyle | Adequate, restful sleep of 7 to 8 hours · Alcohol or smoking, and how often · Travel for work, how often and for how long · Overstressed on most days |
| Food | Vegetarian, vegan, eggetarian or non-vegetarian · Food or lifestyle habits to change |
| Medical | Any diagnosed condition · Current medications |
| Values | Top three |
| Strengths | Top three |

All twenty are free text today. After the mock call both of them concluded it
should be multiple choice (Round 2, item 5).

### 2. The goal-setting session

In the order her worksheet runs:

1. **What was shared.** The intake answers, in front of her.
2. **Vision and values.** The client's own picture of where they want to be,
   and their three values read back.
3. **The wellness wheel.** Four physical dimensions — exercise and movement,
   nutrition, sleep, lifestyle (chronic stress, alcohol) — and "the dimensions
   with the largest gap from your vision". After the mock she decided to take
   the wheel out of the first session (Round 2, item 9).
4. **Summary.** Three or four key themes drawn from the answers.
5. **One row per dimension.** Physical activity, nutrition, sleep, stress,
   lifestyle, each with goals, pain points and barriers.
6. **Top three strengths**, and what the client asks of her.
7. **Health goal for three to six months.** One primary, one secondary.
8. **Behaviour goals.** A grid: exercise, nutrition, sleep and lifestyle
   across, up to three down. Nutrition is left open until the food log and the
   blood report are in.
9. **Barriers the client foresees.** Up to four, each with a way round it that
   uses one of their strengths.
10. **Additional discussion.** Any medical condition that needs clearance or a
    changed plan (her examples: thyroid, PCOS, diabetes). For women, life stage
    and whether periods are regular. The physical assessment. The exercise slot
    that suits them best.

### 3. After the call: what she asks for

- A food log of three or four days — food, liquids and water — and how many
  times a week they eat out or order in.
- The most recent blood test report, if there is one.
- A note to say to themselves every day that reflects their vision and goal. A
  vision board can help.

## The physical assessment

One table, measured three times: today, after a month and a half, and after
three months.

| Group | What she measures |
| --- | --- |
| Health | BMI · Waist circumference · Waist-to-hip ratio · Resting heart rate |
| Strength | Number of squats |
| Stamina | Step test or brisk walk (undecided) |
| Flexibility | Sit and reach · Apley scratch test |
| Stability | Y balance test |
| Posture | Static posture |
| How they feel | Sleep · Energy · Gut (acidity, bloating, constipation) · Period regularity · Any pain · Mood swings |
| From the blood report | Other metabolic numbers "with concern" |

Her sheet writes a reference beside two rows: waist under 36 inches, and
waist-to-hip between 0.7 and 0.85. Those are hers to hold and to talk about.
Whether the app ever shows a member a reference beside her own number is a
scope question and not a layout one (`CLAUDE.md`, Scope of practice). It is on
the list in `docs/NEXT-STEPS.md`.

## Where the gaps sit

1. **The app does not ask what she asks.** Of her twenty intake questions,
   three are asked the way she asks them. Eight of the rest have a field or a
   near-equivalent somewhere, and nine have nowhere to go.
2. **Goals have no order and no measure.** She sets one primary and one
   secondary for three to six months. The app has a list of sentences and a
   twelve-week journey.
3. **Nothing joins a behaviour to the goal it serves, or a barrier to its way
   round.** The pieces exist (focus lines, module actions, "Not today" reasons)
   without the thread.
4. **There is no assessment to repeat.** Her three-column table has no home, so
   "after a month and a half" has nothing to be compared with.

None of this is built. What to build, and in what order, is the conversation
`docs/NEXT-STEPS.md` sets up.
